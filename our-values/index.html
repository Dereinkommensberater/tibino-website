What we stand for

A children's series teaches something whether it means to or not. These are the things ours teaches on purpose.

Kindness before cleverness. In a Tibino story, nobody wins by being the smartest or the fastest. Things turn out well because somebody noticed that a friend needed help — and because everyone else joined in.

The five that shape every episode

  • Kindness before clevernessBeing clever is fine. Being kind is what the story rewards.
  • Help that arrivesWhen someone is in trouble, help comes — quickly and from more than one direction. Children are building a picture of how the world responds; we would like that picture to be a reliable one.
  • Patience with someone smallerMilo the kitten hides. Nobody drags him out. They sit nearby and wait, and eventually he comes. Waiting for someone is a value we show far more often than we say.
  • Courage without dangerStorm walks into the river because a kitten needs him — not to prove anything, and never in real peril. Courage here means doing the useful thing while you are still a bit unsure.
  • BelongingA stranger arrives and is simply taken in. No test, no earning it, no conditions.

What we leave out on purpose

No villains. There is no bad character in any episode. Problems happen by accident — a basket drifts off, someone gets lost — so a child never has to sort the world into good people and bad people.

No punishment and no shaming. Nobody is told off, laughed at or made small. Pip is nervous about almost everything and that is never the joke.

No sarcasm. Under about six, children hear the words and miss the tone. A sarcastic line lands as a mean one.

No rivalry as a motor. Nobody competes for who is best, first or favourite.

Why the characters never change their look

Each figure is designed once and then kept visually identical in every episode — same colours, same markings, same eyes. It sounds like a production detail, but for a two-year-old it is the difference between a friend and a stranger. Recognising someone is where trust in a story begins, and we spend a lot of effort protecting it.

Why the endings are quiet

Every episode ends calm: everyone together, warm light, nothing unresolved. Many children's shows end on a spike to hold you into the next video. We would rather your child can stop after one — and that the last thing they saw was gentle.
