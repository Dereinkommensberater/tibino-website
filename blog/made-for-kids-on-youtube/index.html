What does “Made for Kids” mean on YouTube?

It is one of the most misread labels on the platform. It says a great deal about your child's data, and almost nothing about the quality of the video.

Short answer. “Made for Kids” is a legal designation under children's privacy law. On such videos YouTube switches off personalised advertising, comments, notifications, saving to playlists and several other features. It does not mean the video has been checked for quality or age-suitability — that judgement is still yours.

Where the label comes from

It exists because of children's privacy law — principally COPPA in the United States, with similar rules elsewhere. Platforms may not collect personal data from children under 13 for advertising purposes, so YouTube requires every creator to declare whether a video is directed at children.

The declaration is made by the uploader, and YouTube's systems check it. Getting it wrong carries real consequences for the channel, which is why most children's creators mark everything this way.

What it switches off

FeatureOn a Made for Kids video
Personalised advertisingOff
CommentsOff
Notifications to subscribersOff
Save to playlist / Watch laterOff
Live chatOff
End screens and cardsOff

For parents the first two lines are the important ones: no profile is built from your child's viewing for ad targeting, and there is no comment section for them to encounter.

What it does not tell you

It is not a quality mark. A video can be marked Made for Kids and still be far too fast, badly made, or unsuitable for your particular child. Nobody at YouTube has watched it and judged it appropriate.

It is also not an age rating. A label meant for a ten-year-old and one meant for a two-year-old carry exactly the same designation.

So the label is genuinely useful for one question — how is my child's data handled — and useless for the other — is this any good. Use it as a filter, then judge the content yourself.

How it differs from YouTube Kids

  • Made for Kids is a property of a single video on ordinary YouTube.
  • YouTube Kids is a separate app with its own filtering, parental controls, timers and the option to disable search entirely.
  • The two overlap but are not the same: a Made for Kids video may appear on ordinary YouTube, and YouTube Kids draws on a curated subset.

For under-fives, the app plus a self-chosen channel list is the stronger setup. The label alone leaves the recommendation rail in charge.

What does it mean if your YouTube video is made for kids?

It is a legal declaration under the US children's privacy law, not a quality mark. Marking a video this way switches off personalised advertising, comments, notifications, saving to playlists and the miniplayer. The label says the video is aimed at children — it says nothing about whether the content is any good.

Should I select Not made for kids on YouTube?

Only if the video genuinely is not aimed at children. The setting is a declaration to a regulator, and getting it wrong carries real consequences for the channel. Judge by the content itself: subject matter, characters, language and who it was made for — not by which setting earns more.

Frequently asked

Does Made for Kids mean there are no ads?

No. It means no personalised advertising. Non-personalised ads based on the video's context can still appear. YouTube Kids shows fewer ads, and a Premium subscription removes them entirely.

Why can't I save a kids' video to a playlist?

Because saving to playlists is one of the features disabled on Made for Kids videos, for privacy reasons. It is a common source of confusion — the video is not broken.

Is Made for Kids the same as an age rating?

No. It is a single yes-or-no privacy designation with no age bands. A video for a ten-year-old and one for a toddler carry the same label.

Can I trust that a Made for Kids video is suitable?

Not automatically. The label describes data handling, not content quality. Watch a minute of any new channel yourself before it becomes a regular.
