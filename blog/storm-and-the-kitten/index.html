EPISODE 3

Storm and the Kitten

A basket drifts down the river with a tiny kitten inside — and the farm gains a new friend called Milo.

What happens: On a sunny day by the river, Tibino, Pip, Foxy and Storm spot a wicker basket drifting downstream. Inside is a cold, frightened kitten. Storm goes into the water without hesitating; the others use a stick and a floating log to bring the basket to shore. The kitten is dried, given warm milk, and given a name: Milo.

How we tell a rescue without frightening anyone

A rescue story has a built-in problem: the thing that makes it gripping for an adult is exactly the thing that makes it upsetting for a two-year-old. Our rule is that help is always visibly on the way. The audience never sits with the danger alone.

In practice that means the friends notice the basket in the very first moments, Storm is already moving before anyone has time to worry, and the water is shown as shallow and calm. There is no swelling music, no slow motion, no moment where the film asks a child to fear the worst. The tension in the episode comes from can we reach it, never from will something terrible happen.

The value it carries

Helping someone who cannot help themselves. Milo is too small and too frightened to do anything for himself. Nobody asks whether he deserves help or whose fault it was — they simply go. Afterwards, nobody is congratulated. The reward in the story is that the kitten is warm.

There is a second, quieter value in the last third: patience. Once Milo is safe, he hides. The others do not pull him out or coax him loudly. They sit near him, and eventually he comes out on his own and purrs. For a child who is sometimes shy, that scene says something useful.

What your child picks up

  • Words that sit on a picture: wet, dry, cold, warm, gentle, carry, hold.
  • Animal names and sounds: puppy, kitten, chick, fox — with the sounds attached.
  • A working sequence: notice → act → it helps. Repeated three times in one episode by three different characters.
  • How to be around someone frightened: get low, stay quiet, wait.

The characters in this one

Storm gets his moment here — he is the one who goes into the water. Foxy stops showing off and becomes useful, which is his whole arc in miniature. Pip is nervous about the water and stays on the bank, and that is completely fine; nobody teases him for it. Milo arrives and stays.

Good to know before you press play

  • Runs about five minutes — a little longer than our usual, because the drying, the milk and the naming all needed their own quiet beats.
  • Calm ending, warm evening light, everyone together. Works before a nap.
  • Open thread: right at the end, Milo looks back at the river. Nothing frightening — but it is the question the next episodes pick up.
