Ad-free videos for kids: the real options

Small children cannot tell an advert from a programme. That single fact is why this question comes up so often.

Short answer. Options are: YouTube Kids (fewer ads), YouTube Premium (none), broadcaster apps such as BBC iPlayer or PBS Kids (none), downloaded episodes (none), or a website that embeds nothing. “Made for Kids” removes personalised advertising but not all advertising.

Why it matters more at this age

The ability to recognise advertising as persuasion develops gradually, and below about seven or eight most children do not reliably distinguish an advert from the programme around it. To a three-year-old, a toy commercial is simply another short film about a toy.

That is the reason children's advertising is regulated more tightly than most, and the reason unboxing videos are a particular concern — they are advertising wearing the costume of play.

The most common misunderstanding: Made for Kids does not mean ad-free. It means no personalised advertising — no profile of your child is used to target them. Contextual ads can still appear.

The formats that are advertising in disguise

  • Unboxing and surprise-toy videos.
  • “Let's play with” videos featuring a single branded product.
  • Channels built around a toy line, where every episode features the merchandise.
  • Sponsored segments inside otherwise ordinary videos, which are frequently not signposted in a way a child could notice.

None of these are removed by an ad-blocker or a Premium subscription, because they are the content rather than an interruption to it.

Frequently asked

Does YouTube Kids have ads?

Yes, though fewer than the main app and never personalised. YouTube Premium removes them. Broadcaster apps such as CBeebies and PBS Kids carry none at all.

At what age do children understand advertising?

Recognising that an advert intends to persuade develops gradually and is generally not reliable before about seven or eight. Younger children can often identify that something is an advert without understanding its purpose.

Are unboxing videos advertising?

In effect, yes, whether or not they are paid. They are built around desire for a product, and preschool children have no way to recognise that framing.

Can I block ads on YouTube Kids?

Not directly within the app. A Premium subscription removes advertising across YouTube including Kids. Downloading episodes in advance is the other reliable route.
